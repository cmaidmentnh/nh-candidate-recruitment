How this works, and what it cannot show

Nobody can read a rival's ad account. This page reads Meta's public Ad Library, which is the whole of what the law makes them publish.

Turn it on {{ 'Ready' if has_token else 'Not set' }}

The archive answers only people Meta has checked the ID of. One token, in META_AD_LIBRARY_TOKEN, serves the whole page. If it is missing, the server key (META_ADS_TOKEN) is tried instead, but Meta may refuse a system-user token here and want a person's.{% if token_source %} Right now it is reading {{ token_source }}.{% endif %}

  1. Confirm your identity and location at facebook.com/ID. This is the only real gate, and Meta says it takes a few days. There is nothing to switch on in the app dashboard afterwards.
  2. Once Meta confirms you, generate a user access token from your Meta for Developers app while signed in as that confirmed account.
  3. Set that token as META_AD_LIBRARY_TOKEN in the server's .env, then restart the app.
  4. Come back here, click Watch a page, and use Find their Facebook Page.

Read the numbers carefully
  • Only political ads are in here. A page that never got authorised to run political ads shows nothing at all. That is not a fault on this page.
  • Spend is a bracket, and the figure shown is priced off impressions. Meta's bottom band is "under $100" with no floor, so averaging the bands overstates any page running many small boosts. Instead each page's cost per thousand impressions is worked out from its own larger ads, and its small ones are priced at that rate, then pinned inside the band Meta published. Meta's band is printed under every figure.
  • For one page's real total, open its Ad Library page and scroll to Disclaimers. Meta prints an actual figure there, per funder, plus last week's spend. The page name in the table links straight to it. Type those in with Edit.
  • This race only. The archive keeps seven years, so a plain search drags in the 2024 cycle. Everything here is cut off at {{ cycle_start|fmt_date(true) }} (META_AD_CYCLE_START).
  • Each ad shows a total for its whole run, not a daily one. The chart is built from one reading a day taken here, so it only goes back to the day the watch started.
  • Search words can catch other people's ads. Once a Page is linked, only that Page's ads count towards their spend.

Everything here is public data that Meta is required to publish, read through the official Ad Library API. No account of theirs is touched, and nothing is done on their Pages.
